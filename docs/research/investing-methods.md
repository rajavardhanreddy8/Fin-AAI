# From celebrated investors to a testable small-cap research process

*Research memo — 11 September 2026. Scope: personal research on India-listed small-cap and growth equities, with roughly INR 10,000 initially and INR 2,000 added monthly. This is a research design, not a return promise, recommendation, or order-execution system.*

## What the primary sources actually support

The transferable core of Warren Buffett's method is business underwriting. In Berkshire's June 1996 *Owner's Manual*, shareholders are framed as part-owners; success is per-share growth in intrinsic business value, and preferred businesses consistently earn above-average returns on capital. The 1987 shareholder letter, dated 29 February 1988, says a purchase should be judged by the business's economics, managers, and price, with market quotations useful as optional offers rather than instructions. The 2007 letter, dated 29 February 2008, makes the quality test more concrete: understandability, durable economics, trustworthy management, and a sensible price. It distinguishes businesses that grow earnings with little incremental capital from rapid growers that consume capital at poor returns.

This is a coherent decision process, but Berkshire's record does not prove that any screen copying its vocabulary will outperform. Buffett had unusual access, judgment, deal structures, insurance float, tax circumstances, concentration tolerance, and a very long horizon. The repeatable elements are questions and accounting relationships: growth in owner earnings per share; return on incremental capital; reinvestment runway; competitive durability; management's treatment of outside shareholders; and price relative to conservative value. The celebrity narrative and selected winners are subject to hindsight and survivorship.

Peter Lynch's Fidelity webcast transcript from 3 November 2022 offers a complementary discovery method. Familiar products or local observations can generate leads, but he describes researching the company, its economics, competition, balance sheet, financial condition, and expansion runway. Thus "what you know" is an idea source, not evidence. His examples are memorable winners chosen with hindsight; an agent should record every lead and rejection, rather than learn only from anecdotes that survived.

Howard Marks adds the missing market and risk layer. *The Most Important Thing* (1 July 2003) emphasizes limits to macro knowledge and loss avoidance. *The Illusion of Knowledge* (8 September 2022) argues that a useful macro call requires both the event and the market response to be forecast better than consensus. *What Really Matters?* (22 November 2022) shifts attention from short-term events and volatility toward long-term value, risk, and return asymmetry. The operational lesson is to use macro conditions for scenario stress and risk posture, never as the sole buy signal. A good company can still be a poor investment when expectations embedded in its price are too demanding.

Academic evidence supports a restrained quality prior, not a formulaic guarantee. Novy-Marx's April 2010 working paper reports that gross profits-to-assets predicted average U.S. stock returns with power comparable to book-to-market and improved value strategies. Asness, Frazzini, and Pedersen's *Quality Minus Junk* (paper dated 5 June 2017) defines quality across profitability, growth, safety, and payout and reports historical risk-adjusted returns in the United States and 24-country data. Yet its broad sample ends in 2012, implements monthly rebalanced long-short portfolios, and is not an India small-cap retail strategy. Linnainmaa and Roberts (December 2016) then show that most examined accounting anomalies weaken sharply outside their discovery samples and warn about backfill and data-snooping. Quality metrics therefore belong in a multi-evidence underwriting process and must pass India-specific, point-in-time tests after realistic costs.

India's market structure changes what is practical. NSE defines impact cost as execution degradation versus an ideal mid-price and notes that it varies by order size, side, and current order book. Low rupee share price says nothing about cheapness: valuation depends on the price of the whole equity claim—market capitalization or enterprise value—relative to earnings, cash flows, assets, and risk. A INR 20 share can be expensive after dilution; a INR 2,000 share can be inexpensive on per-share value. Fractional-share availability is an execution constraint, not valuation evidence.

SEBI's current LODR master circular (30 January 2026) is the disclosure backbone, while its surveillance page links current ASM/GSM status, promoter pledges, insolvency status, price-band changes, and rumour clarifications. These are evidence feeds, not automatic accusations: surveillance status can be triggered by market parameters, but it warrants explicit review. For banks, ordinary industrial-company measures such as gross profit, working capital, net debt/EBITDA, and free cash flow are economically ill-specified. RBI's 1 April 2025 rules make loan classification, cash recovery, provisioning, and regulatory capital central. Bank analysis must use asset quality, provision coverage, capital adequacy, deposit/franchise economics, concentration, and credit costs in a separate model.

## Deterministic research-agent design

Each agent should return a typed record, not persuasive prose. Every claim needs: `issuer_id`, `metric_or_claim`, `value`, `unit`, `period_end`, `public_timestamp`, `source_url`, `source_document`, `page_or_table`, `calculation`, `confidence`, and `contrary_evidence`. Missing data stays null; it is never silently imputed. Every output carries a versioned methodology and an `as_of` timestamp. Research snapshots must preserve delisted names, corporate actions, restatements, and the securities actually knowable and tradable on that date.

1. **Universe and evidence agent.** Build the India-listed universe as it existed on each date. Resolve ISIN/ticker changes, listing board, trading status, corporate actions, filing timestamps, ASM/GSM, insolvency, promoter pledge, and price bands. Reject a backtest if constituents come from today's survivor list or a financial statement becomes available at its period-end date. The contract should include an evidence-completeness score and a list of stale or conflicting filings.

2. **Accounting-normalization agent.** Route issuers first to `bank`, `other_financial`, or `nonfinancial`. For nonfinancials, reconstruct revenue, gross profit where meaningful, operating cash flow, maintenance/total capex, working capital, net debt, dilution, and returns on capital across at least a cycle. Reconcile cash flow with earnings and flag auditor qualifications, restatements, abrupt receivable/inventory growth, capitalized costs, related-party balances, and repeated exceptional items. For banks, use the RBI-specific schema above and never rank them on nonfinancial free-cash-flow formulas.

3. **Business-quality agent.** Convert Buffett/Lynch questions into falsifiable fields: revenue driver, customer dependency, unit economics, reinvestment mechanism, addressable runway, switching or cost advantage, evidence that the advantage persists, and conditions that end the thesis. It must distinguish observation from inference and name the closest competing explanation. Product familiarity may open a case; it cannot pass one.

4. **Governance agent.** Create a time series of promoter ownership and pledges, related-party transactions, remuneration, preferential issues, warrants, dilution, auditor changes/qualifications, board independence, capital allocation, and divergence between stated targets and delivered results. Emit red flags with direct filing evidence. It should not infer fraud from a single anomaly; unresolved material conflicts block a high-confidence rating.

5. **Valuation and expectations agent.** Produce bear/base/bull owner-earnings scenarios with explicit revenue, margin, reinvestment, dilution, terminal economics, and discount-rate assumptions. Show value per share and market capitalization, plus reverse valuation: what growth and margins the current price requires. Compare multiples only within economically comparable business models. No single precise target is allowed; the output is a range and the variables that drive it.

6. **Skeptic and risk agent.** Independently attack the thesis: identify cyclicality disguised as growth, customer or supplier concentration, refinancing needs, regulatory exposure, governance failure modes, disruption, and permanent-loss paths. Macro variables enter as stresses—rates, currency, commodity inputs, or recession—not forecasts. It returns disconfirming evidence and a pre-mortem.

7. **Liquidity and portfolio agent.** Estimate spread, depth/impact for the intended rupee order, trading frequency, and days needed to exit under normal and stressed volume. It budgets all broker and statutory costs using the schedule effective on the trade date. It may recommend `research`, `watch`, or `eligible`; it cannot place orders. With INR 10,000, apparent diversification into many tiny positions can be defeated by lot prices, costs, and shallow liquidity. The monthly INR 2,000 contribution is better treated as a periodic decision budget than an obligation to buy.

8. **Decision-audit agent.** Freeze the thesis, valuation range, evidence version, expected holding period, kill conditions, and unresolved questions before any human decision. Score the forecast later against business outcomes, not just price. Maintain rejected cases and failures so evaluation includes the full opportunity set rather than a gallery of winners.

## Proposed rules to validate, not sourced facts

The following are conservative starting hypotheses and must be tested on point-in-time Indian data; none comes from the cited investors as a numeric rule. Require at least five fiscal years where available, no unresolved material filing conflict, and a downside case showing financing survival without optimistic capital raising. Flag rather than automatically reject any promoter pledge, auditor resignation, repeated equity issuance, or related-party transaction above 5% of annual revenue or assets. Require intended order value to be no more than 5% of median daily traded value and modeled round-trip impact plus charges below 1%; tighten after observing real quotes. Cap an initial company at 10% of invested capital and an economic theme at 25%, while recognizing that a INR 10,000 account may need fewer holdings or a diversified fund core to avoid uneconomic fragments. Keep cash when no candidate clears governance, liquidity, and valuation gates.

Validate with walk-forward tests that use filing-publication timestamps, original values available then, contemporaneous universes including later delistings, corporate-action-adjusted returns, and transaction costs scaled to order size. Report median outcomes, drawdowns, turnover, capacity, and results by liquidity bucket; compare against a simple investable India benchmark and equal-cash timing. Freeze thresholds before the holdout period and cut any attractive historical result substantially when setting expectations. Paper-test the full research and monitoring loop for at least six months before considering any broker integration.

Practical limits remain severe. Public filings can reveal inconsistency but cannot prove promoter honesty or moat durability. Thin order books change quickly. Indian small-cap histories are short, regimes change, and vendor-cleaned historical data may overwrite restatements or omit dead firms. A INR 2,000 monthly flow cannot reliably implement institutional long-short factors or broad rebalancing. The system's defensible edge is disciplined rejection, traceable evidence, lower behavioral error, and better use of limited research time—not mechanical imitation of famous investors or guaranteed excess return.
